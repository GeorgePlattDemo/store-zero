## 1. Purpose and exact scope

The proposed Scan-to-Build service begins with work a customer needs, not with an operator programming an isolated part in CAD/CAM. The question for Project 1 is whether a **complete, versioned customer definition** can reach a yard's independently evaluated material and modeled machine work without a skilled intermediary quietly reinterpreting the request or a user-facing page manufacturing a plausible answer. The software path is bounded: an unsupported operation must be refused; a different customer revision must obtain a fresh answer; a positive digital result must not become physical Cycle Start authorization.

The specimen is **two dimensional 2×4 braces**, finished as parallel face-miter components with one identified shallow pointed-drill location on each part. Their working geometry, stock, machining requirements, operation identities and costs are checked at the boundary rather than inferred from a picture. The standard demonstration is 16 inches; a permitted 18-inch choice changes the derived angle, feature coordinates, material requirement and Store's parent selection. A third reference case uses selected spot positions and **independently different depths** to prove the machine planner consumes those exact operands.

This manuscript is not a claims brochure, a replacement specification or an alternative computation. It is the reader-facing interpretation of the existing source, calculation, accepted packets and records. The owner of a fact is cited at the point it enters the path, and the unfavorable path is part of the proof.

**What it establishes:** identified demand and independent coverage; current reference Store computations and operation ledgers; binding of a simulated acceptance to the evaluated Store response; verification and lowering to registered modeled operations; virtual replay; zero physical motion permission. **What it does not establish:** correct physical saw kerf-face location, reliable roller grip, guard/interlock validation, controller compilation, measured production time, available retail inventory, completed sale, completed parts or inspected dimensions.

## 3. Three owners, one unbroken path

The architecture has three non-overlapping owners. [Program](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/3d-solutions-program) owns the research question, economics, proposed lab work and reasons to investigate local value creation. [System](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/new-system) owns the customer's definition, shared operational meaning, revision, independent manifest, translation, transport, decision and owner-held record. [Store](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/store-zero) owns what a particular yard can provide: catalog, represented stock, registered capability, whole-job planning, predicted occupancy, Q, receipt, refusal and machine lowering. The commissioned physical apparatus will require a separate local operator authority.

The observable path is:

`Idea (unnumbered intake) → Intent → The bench → Published definition → Independent manifest → System translation → Same-origin Store transport → Fresh Store evaluation → Store answer + receipt → Your call → Exact accepted packet → Store verification and lowering → Virtual motion records and replay → Blocked physical admission`.

`Idea` captures source material; `Intent` establishes the job facts. The six-step public sequence is **Intent → The bench → The Store answers → Your call → We cut it → Pick up & build**. The latter cutting and collection stages are narrated/simulated in the public demonstration. They do not assert that a saw operated or that a buyer paid.

The information boundary is crucial. System cannot assign a Store SKU, precompute the Store price, select a stock parent, invent a receipt or promote an unsupported feature into a supported one. Store cannot silently change the customer's required angle, spot locations, depths, grade or finished dimensions to find a cheaper path. The operator cannot mistake the accepted digital packet for permission to energize a physical machine. The source-of-truth relationship is visible in [System's published wire](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/new-system/blob/main/src/system/store/published-wire.ts), [Store's request owner](src/requests/store-request.mjs) and [Store's machine evidence](src/machine/evidence.mjs).

## 4. Customer requirement: the part before the machine

The customer names a dimensional component outcome. The job's components are `PART-1` and `PART-2`; each has its own `SPOT-1` or `SPOT-2` and must remain identifiable in the Store request, plan and result. The nominal material form is board with nominal thickness 2 and width 4 (actual modeled section 1.5 × 3.5 inches). The public demonstration starts with `spf`, a declared 2×4 stock class, 16-inch finished part length, 8-inch cross span, parallel face-miter ends, and one shallow, centered-on-wide-face spot per part. The equivalent 18-inch configuration preserves the fixed 8-inch span while deriving its own angle and center positions. The user does **not** choose a specific Store parent board.

An owner's *requirement* is not a machine-control instruction. `PART-1` length 18 inches and center spot X=9 inches are statements about the required workpiece. Commands such as `MOVE C ...`, which parent SKU to load, tool speed, or how to establish an actual retained face are manufacturing resolutions that must come from registered machinery and the Store response, not from the user's form.

The source contract also preserves `endRelation: parallel` and `lengthDatum: long-long-outer-edge`. That signed physical meaning matters: an angled cut measured to a different face is a **different** requirement. No general word such as “bevel” may stand in for the actual required plane and datum. The reference behavior is neither a freehand measurement nor a CAD drawing regenerated by the yard employee.

Material is a genuine customer choice. SPF is a convenient initial species, not a universal price or an assumed material if the user changes to treated southern yellow pine. Treated choices require an explicit grade, including `above-ground`, `ground-contact` and `ground-contact-cedartone` where offered. An incomplete grade requirement cannot be silently substituted with a default.

## 5. Derivation of the two-brace definition

For the 2×4 parallel-miter brace geometry, the specified cross span is 8.000 inches and the customer can vary finished brace length **L** over the supported discrete range. System derives the miter magnitude using

\[\theta(L)=\arcsin\!\left(\frac{8}{L}\right)\frac{180}{\pi}\quad\text{degrees}.\]

Thus a 16-inch brace yields exactly 30 degrees and an 18-inch brace yields 26.387799961243 degrees (rounded in the current serialized request). If the centered spot rule is selected, the part-relative longitudinal feature coordinate is **L/2**: 8 inches for both 16-inch parts, and 9 inches for both 18-inch parts. The wide-face location is centered rather than a vague “somewhere in the middle.” The two identified parts, two spots, three saw events including the initial reference, and requested parent-dependent stock envelope remain part of a complete job.

This derivation belongs to System's project rule, not the Store's price engine. System passes the resolved geometrical meaning and every required feature in one named revision; the parent selection and nominal station transforms then belong to Store. Supported changes cannot obtain the old result merely because they reuse the same page, session or description.

**A change in definition is an economic change opportunity, not a license to rewrite the engineering.** Sixteen to eighteen inches changes both the derived angle and part lengths. That may change usable stock patterns, parent length, stock Q, longitudinal travel, angle motion and drilling coordinates. The current supported outcomes are recorded in chapters 8 and 11, not assumed from the price printed next to an old control.

## 6. Independent manifest and admission

Admission asks whether the entire definition is meaningful and complete enough to ask a Store; it does not decide whether that Store can make the work. The request carries an independently constructed manifest identifying the components and every demanded feature. Store checks coverage so that `SUPPORTABLE` means *every required item was answered*, not that at least one inexpensive or easy item found a path. A missing or contradicted requirement must be rejected, refused or kept unresolved with a reason.

A complete standard inquiry needs the request class `USER_DEFINED_BOARD_V1`, an identified revision/configuration, material species/form/nominal section and any required grade, defined parts and finished lengths, miter plane/end relation and length datum, the angle and the identified spots, and a complete independent manifest. No post-acceptance step may add an omitted tool, change the part width or drop an inconvenient depth. An unsupported special feature is not silently changed into a generic shallow spot.

The exact canonical serialized request, manifest and response for each current reference specimen are in [current-proof.json](docs/project-1-digital-trail/current-proof.json). They are deliberately **not recreated by hand here as a second machine-readable authority**; the full field-by-field record is linked with its input identity and echoed in the operation/price tables below. Anyone auditing coverage should compare that request, manifest, reply and accepted packet under one specimen ID rather than splice together attractive fields from unrelated runs.

## 7. The actual System-to-Store boundary

The public browser submits its Store inquiry to the System's same-origin `/api/store-zero/job` endpoint, or `/api/store-zero/offering` for discovery. These routes call the single [published-wire translator](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/new-system/blob/main/src/system/store/published-wire.ts). That translator preserves the defined geometry/operands, establishes the independent manifest, refuses incomplete published demands, and sends the complete Store-owned request to the configured `STORE_ZERO_ORIGIN` endpoint. System's `STORE_CANDIDATE` records the specifically inspected Store commit, required protocol and machine-configuration hash. An inquiring browser cannot choose a different authoritative Store pin.

The Store service implements `/health`, `/v1/requests`, and `/v1/machine-evidence` independently from System. It evaluates the request against the catalog loaded for that inquiry and replies with its own answer and receipt. System validates the returned release, request and response identities, and passes it back to the published UI. Store refusals must reach the same visible route; transport failure cannot become a fabricated favorable estimate.

The [System CI workflow](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/new-system/actions/workflows/verify.yml) starts **two separate processes**, explicitly checking out Store at `10d36c09ff6e0dac3c098a004821efea81a2b58d`. It exercises the real System page and exact pinned Store over HTTP. The Railway candidate project likewise declares separate System and Store services, with the inter-service origin supplied to System's server. Separation of responsibility and release identity are concrete here; a Railway service connection is **not, by itself, a verified network firewall**. Live hostname exposure and actual deployment revisions require their own checks.

## 8. Store catalog and complete stock evaluation

The current standard 16-inch SPF case is resolved by the Store onto a single **60-inch** nominal 2×4 SPF parent, `STB-ZERO-SPF-2X4-60-001`, represented at **$2.61** material selling price. The 18-inch variation requires the **72-inch** parent `STB-ZERO-SPF-2X4-72-001`, represented at **$3.13**. These are Store-selected parents, not commands entered by the customer or hardcoded in the browser. The selected-location/depth variant retains the 72-inch SPF parent but has distinct feature operands and machine service.

The operation search must reconcile *whole-job* demand, not merely price a separate board for each part. Saw allowances, parent patterns, retained material, registered processing reach and safe-support screens are examined alongside the catalog and modeled inventory. A service can return a bounded feasible solution with `optimization.searchComplete: false`; that is not proof of whole-domain optimality. A complete Q requires complete manifest coverage. Material that is unavailable, has unverified grade, cannot retain sufficient support or needs an unregistered operation must not be processed by omitting inconvenient requirements.

The 18-inch historical example in the sealed review uses a different treated-lumber catalog and Stage-2 selection, not the present SPF parent. The distinction is deliberate and traceable: demand determines the material class; Store-selected parent, service time and Q follow *that* demand at *that* identified Store revision. Chapter 16 gives the comparison's exact non-equivalence.

## 9. Registered dimensional machine and coordinate meaning

Store's adopted modeled D-001 cell is established through [machine configuration](data/machine/manufacturing-core-0.1.json), [model construction](src/machine/configuration.mjs) and the [manufacturing core](src/evaluation/engine/store-manufacturing-core.mjs). It is registered **before** the specific customer's definition is lowered. Registration identifies operations, tool geometry, station positions, datums, units, signed transforms, the predicted motion/timing parameters, stock and processing envelopes, and refusal conditions. A registered model is not a calibrated machine.

| Registered modeled function | Declared fact | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Handling | 36 s load-and-seat; 24 s release-and-label per parent | Model occupancy, not observed operator work |
| Longitudinal motion | X/C 8 in/s, 32 in/s² | Nominal stock-index travel |
| Across-width motion | Y 4 in/s, 16 in/s² | Registered tool traverse/placement |
| Miter angle | A 30 degrees/s, 120 degrees/s² | Axis A is the angle axis, **not** fence datum A |
| Saw stroke | S rapid 6 in/s, 24 in/s² | Nominal geometric stroke, not saw commissioning |
| Spot vertical | Z rapid 2 in/s, 16 in/s² | Approach/plunge/retract motion basis |
| Saw model | 20-in blade, 1,800 rpm, 80 teeth, 0.003 in/tooth, 0.5 finish factor | Declared processing benchmark |
| Kerf and reach | 0.125-in nominal kerf, up to 45° supported angle | Requires real cut-face calibration before production |
| Fixed stations | Saw nominal X=0; spot nominal X=36 in; modeled rollers X=24/48 in | Positions used in declared transforms and overlap screens |
| Spot tool | 3/16-in pointed drill, 118° point, 3,000 rpm, 0.008 in/rev | 24 in/min modeled feed; maximum 3/16-in full-diameter penetration |

The machine uses three distinct conceptual datums. **Fence (datum A)** establishes side contact and across-width placement. **Support plane (datum B)** establishes actual section height and plunge reference. **Workpiece reference (datum C)** relates component length and feature positions to fixed stations as material advances. The modeled move axis `C` is a local travel convention, not a command to an established rotary machine axis. A source design's informal words must not overwrite these registered mechanical meanings.

A spot at part-relative X = `x_f` reaches a station at `x_t` when the nominal material reference is offset by `C = x_t − x_f`, subject to retained-face and kerf conventions. Thus the 16-inch first spot nominally moves C from 0 to 28 when the station is at 36 and the feature is 8 inches from the start. The 18-inch corresponding first spot uses C=27; the selected first spot X=5.25 uses C=30.75. Later moves also include cut, reference and retained kerf conventions and must be copied from the actual Store ledger, not recomputed casually from one formula.

**Physical correspondence remains open.** Kerf removes material, leaving two different faces. The reference must identify the retained face, not just the blade center or a pleasing zero display. Two support rollers appearing within a nominal interval does not prove force, grip, material retention or resistance to cut loads. At some positions, one roller can lie outside the remaining stock envelope. An artificial delay labeled `CLAMP_ALLOWANCE` or `VERIFY_POSITION_ALLOWANCE` is not a clamp sensor or verification of physical state.

## 10. The spot feature: geometry, not a word

`SPOT_ON_LOCATION` means a bounded shallow feature produced with a **pointed drill**. It does not authorize a generic fastening pilot, flat-bottom spotface, through-drill, or drilling of any requested feature at a convenient default location. The **tool diameter is 0.1875 in**, the included drill-point angle is 118°, and the nominal/maximum *full-diameter* penetration is **0.1875 in beyond the drill tip**. Each feature's explicitly requested depth, if present and valid, must drive the modeled Z plunge and time.

The point height follows the actual drill geometry:

\[h_{tip} = \frac{0.1875 / 2}{\tan(118^\circ/2)} \approx 0.056330683\text{ in}.\]

Therefore a named standard spot at 0.1875-in full-diameter depth has modeled total drill-tip plunge `0.243830683 in`; a 0.125-in feature has `0.181330683 in`; and a 0.0625-in feature has `0.118830683 in`. These values must be reflected in the accepted Store operation ledger and lowering, not merely displayed in a descriptive label. The reference planes in the operation tables show Z coordinates as recorded by the current modeled cell; those absolute numbers are **model coordinates**, not measured actual tool positions.

The three current **Store reference** specimens are separate in both position and depth. The 16-inch case spots each part at X=8 and depth 0.1875; the 18-inch centered case uses X=9 and depth 0.1875. The independently varied Store reference case uses part 1 X=5.25/depth 0.125 and part 2 X=10.25/depth 0.0625. System's **published-browser** acceptance currently exposes a shared selected-depth control: it tests both selected X positions with depth **0.125 for both parts**. These are complementary proofs, not identical serialized accepted jobs. The source and acceptance obligations must not falsely declare they are the same specimen.

A negative selected depth, excessive 0.25-in full-diameter depth, wrong drill tool, unsupported flat-bottom demand or missing essential feature is not made supportable by substituting the nominal 0.1875-in operation. The published browser test specifically confirms `SPOT_FULL_DIAMETER_DEPTH_EXCEEDS_MAX` and that a refused revision has no Q or accepted packet authority.

## 11. Modeled time, material, whole-job Q and hashes

For the dimensional Project 1 case, hardware is absent and there are no separately charged panel cuts:

\[S_h = \operatorname{round}_{0.01}(250\,T_h/3600),\qquad Q = \operatorname{round}_{0.01}(M_{raw}+S_h).\]

Hourly service is rounded at the **whole-job service boundary before Q**; do not sum independently rounded parent-support displays as a substitute for the total. ## 12. Customer's decision, accepted packet and custody

After a complete favorable Store answer, the owner may proceed through the public **Your call** step in the demonstration. That is a **simulated commercial acceptance**, not a captured payment or production release. The accepted packet schema is `STB-ACCEPTED-JOB-PACKET-2`; the Store reference builder records the customer revision/manifest, complete Store answer with receipt, project identity, `decision.kind: ACCEPTED`, the simulated decision evidence class and `physicalRelease: false`. The simulated accepted packet belongs to exactly that complete inquiry, not to an earlier estimate or another project.

Its machine evidence does not adopt a caller's edited price, another Store release, a counterfeit receipt or an unsupported operation. The price, parents, operation ledger and predicted seconds already determined by Store are bound into local modeled work; they are not rediscovered by a lowerer, and they are never re-entered in CAD/CAM by a second user.

The **current reference** builder creates deterministic example packets for audit. System's separate **published-browser** test makes the customer use the nested public page, observes the returned HTTP body, invokes the actual commercial acceptance controls, exports the exact packet, and sends it to the pinned Store's machine-evidence endpoint. Evidence created by direct invocation of the Store generator alone cannot establish that the browser preserved the customer's revision or displayed it honestly.

The record must be append-only in its consequential meaning. A changed definition needs a fresh inquiry and new acceptance. Old accepted packets must not be quietly relabeled to cover a later 18-inch setting, a new wood grade or shifted spot coordinates. A refused revision cannot proceed into Your call with the earlier favorable Store answer.

## 13. Verification, lowering and virtual execution

The current Store chain reads `request → answer/receipt → accepted packet → verification → local job → motion records → virtual run → admission`. The maintained reference generator calls `verifyJobPacket`, `lowerJobPacket`, `motionRecords`, `runVirtual`, then `physicalAdmission` for each specimen; its acceptance rejects any specimen that does not verify/lower, replay as `VIRTUAL_MODEL_COMPLETE`, or end with `BLOCKED` admission and **zero permitted physical motion commands**.

The lowerer binds the accepted parents and exact 51-operation Store plan to registered local function/axis meaning. It is a **modeled adapter** that exposes nominal occupied machine operations; it does not select a new stock parent, recalculate Q, silently modify the component geometry, compile TwinCAT Structured Text or produce a commissioned control program. The virtual interpreter consumes that bound record; physical gates remain independent.

The original historical review additionally includes a controller-oriented 45-command reference and virtual state-machine exploration. It was not compiled or run in TwinCAT. That historical command count is **not** the 51-current-operations count, and may not be substituted for an adopted controller implementation. The current modeled `MOVE C`, `MOVE A`, `MOVE S`, `MOVE Y` and `MOVE Z` operations are engineering occupancy records, not assurance of actual motion hardware.

The operation ledgers below reproduce the present Store reference's ordered operations, subjects and predicted seconds in full. They serve as the human-auditable arithmetic trail. The authoritative serialized counterpart remains [current-proof.json](docs/project-1-digital-trail/current-proof.json); the reader can compare the exact `packet.storeAnswer.operations`, `localJob`, `records`, `run` and `admission` for each specimen. ## 16. Material selection, the comparison and its exact limits

The public Job 1 starts in **SPF**, while the preserved engineering review uses **treated southern yellow pine**. A reader who observes different numbers has found a meaningful part of the problem: prices and stock choices belong to a specific material definition, grade, Store revision and catalog, not to the abstract drawing of two braces. The Bench's **Change Wood** tool and Store-backed offering lookup let the customer establish a different material demand. The Store may then return a different parent, service and Q—or ask for information that has not been supplied. There is no authorized universal price for “two braces” detached from that request.

For the original 3 October manuscript, the 18-inch case uses treated SYP **above-ground** stock `STB-ZERO-PTAG-2X4-72-001`, material selling price **$5.15**, modeled machine service **$5.94**, predicted time **85.5001 s**, and historical **Q $11.09** under its pinned Stage-2 evaluator and catalog. The [original manuscript](docs/project-1-digital-trail/D001_Project1_Review.md) contains its separate hashes, the original retained-stock calculation, 45 controller-oriented records and physical gaps. It is preserved without rewriting its conclusion.

A separate **current Store treated-lumber comparison** recorded in [the Store master, §5.17](STORE-ZERO-MASTER.md#517-worked-calculations-and-sensitivities) uses a **$4.81** parent, 86.46953628299116 predicted seconds, **$6.00** service and **Q $10.81**. This is not a reproduction of the historic $11.09 offer: the catalog and model identity differ. The exact grade in that current example is **above-ground**. The catalog separately offers **ground-contact** and **ground-contact-cedartone**, each with its own SKU and selling price. Treating those three grades as interchangeable to force an old number would be precisely the kind of false material admission this architecture is meant to prevent.

The material comparison is worth leaving open for an investigator: choose the job, change its wood, inspect the grade choices and read the *fresh answer*. **Do not hardcode a promise that ground-contact reproduces $11.09.** That live total was not independently observed for this version of the document. If a reviewer tests a particular public choice, its exact definition/grade, pin, receipt, parent SKU, Q and time must become its own identified evidence record before asserting numerical equivalence.

The critical alignment is unaffected by the intentional difference of price/material examples: the meaning of a 16- or 18-inch component, required features, return of all requested work, ownership of selection, receipt identity, lowering, virtual motion meaning and physical refusal must agree throughout the three repositories. Nothing in the demonstration should silently “repair” a price discrepancy by changing an operation, omitting a feature or copying a cached answer.

## 17. Adverse cases, refusals and anti-shortcut evidence

A credible proof must show that the Store is able to say *no*. The published-browser acceptance currently tests a selected full-diameter depth of **0.25 in**, beyond the registered **0.1875-in** maximum. Store returns `REFUSED`, reason code `SPOT_FULL_DIAMETER_DEPTH_EXCEEDS_MAX`, **Q null** and `quoteComplete: false`; retrying the same refused definition yields a distinct request identity but **cannot advance to acceptance**. The previous accepted packet is not authority for this modified revision. The browser's Your call remains disabled for the refusal.

The System bench tests also establish an important treated-material negative case: specifying `syp-treated` 2×4 without grade makes the current job **UNRESOLVED**, reason `GRADE_CHOICE_REQUIRED`, with choices `above-ground`, `ground-contact` and `ground-contact-cedartone`. Store is not allowed to pick one silently merely to print a Q. The test covers a missing material fact that is not even admitted to Store, and another Store pin/receipt mismatch that is rejected. Those are different stages with different remedies.

Other necessary unfavorable cases include unsupported cut planes, impossible stock/retained control, incomplete manifests, foreign release, a changed result total, unknown or wrong machine configuration, absence of a required selected feature, unsupported flat-bottom spotface and a purported physical-release claim. Specific coverage and status must be taken from [Store acceptance tests](acceptance/), [System's published wire tests](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/new-system/blob/main/src/system/store/published-wire.test.ts), [System's bench tests](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/new-system/blob/main/src/system/start-own-bench.test.ts) and CI. **Do not represent all of those cases as observed in one single end-to-end browser run.** Unit/contract tests and the browser prove different parts of the chain.

A refusal does more than reject a purchase: it identifies an unsupported requirement, an unavailable catalog state, incomplete information or an unresolved physical capability. Those distinctions are potential machine-development intelligence. They are not permission to mask a design limitation or promote a proposed machine improvement into today's registered capability.

## 18. Published-browser acceptance versus reference replay

## 19. Physical control and commissioning gates

The D-001 concept is a small registered machine with two downstroke saw functions, stock-reference and restrained-feeding functions, three routing functions and two shallow spotting functions in the broader proposed apparatus. Project 1 exercises the relevant dimensional saw/spot subset in software. The nominal support interval and fixed station geometry are not sufficient to claim safe operation or dimensional conformity.

Before machine motion could be authorized, a local engineering and operator process would have to prove, among other things: exact retained face after each kerf and miter; physical X/C reference capture and encoder/roller relationship; stock grip/support and restraint at every instant, including when one roller no longer contacts the stock; saw stroke envelope and guarding; clamping and tool sensing; drill-point and part-surface offsets; safe offcut capture; axis limits, emergency-stop and interlocks; disturbance/recovery/restart semantics; actual feed/cycle times; and inspection of both labeled brace lengths, miter orientation and drilled feature positions/depths against their original identified customer revision.

The current model's `CONTROL_SCREEN`, `CLAMP_ALLOWANCE`, `VERIFY_POSITION_ALLOWANCE`, `NOMINAL_DATUM_CAPTURE` and `FEATURE_ANSWERED` records do **not** certify those physical facts. The original historical record identified one-roller control and kerf-face correspondence gaps; the current model retains two-roller-contact uncertainty. The local apparatus must be commissioned and inspected in a supervised setting; the software does not bypass that human gate. [Program's supervised lab trial](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/3d-solutions-program/blob/main/SUPERVISED-LABORATORY-CELL-TRIAL.md) records the candidate physical test plan, not a completed installation.

The current `physicalAdmission` result is **BLOCKED** for all three Store reference specimens with **zero permitted motion commands**. `VIRTUAL_MODEL_COMPLETE` is the model's evaluation state. Only appropriate commissioned physical evidence can support later claims about actually cut wood, measured yield, operator safety, local economics and service reliability.
